# Rapid Grok: a game of its own

Ankur, 27 Nov 2026: Rapid Opus feels more polished, but Rapid Grok should not be rebuilt as a copy of it. It stays its own standalone game, with its own ideas, so we can later learn from it and bring the good bits into the main game.

Standing rules still apply: teal and blue neon for you, purple and pink for the enemy. Hulls stay strictly hexagonal.

## What makes it Rapid Grok

This is a fast, one-action-at-a-time fight against the computer. You take a turn, then they take a turn. That pair is one **beat**. It is not both sides acting at once.

The clock is tight: **15 seconds** to act, then **3 seconds** to watch their reply if there is something to see, or if they hit you. Tap the board to skip the watch. If they acted completely out of sight and nothing touched you, the watch is skipped, so you are not made to wait for nothing.

Movement is the other big difference. A Skiff or a Dart can move **up to 3 hexes** in one action, to any reachable hex in that circle. Bigger ships (the Arrow, the Lance, the Flower) can move up to 2. You are not limited to one hex, and there is no “the line” you have to cross before you can turn, pass or scan. There is also **no passing**. If the clock runs out, something still happens: a chosen laser or missile fires after a short red **Cancel**, or a ship moves forward.

You lose if your Flower (the 7-hex flagship) is destroyed. After 150 beats with both Flowers still standing, it is a draw.

When the fight ends, Rapid Grok already has something the main rapid game does not: **Replay**. You can step back through the beats. The log is written in beats, in plain English — your action, then their reply.

The board is its own too: a slightly smaller hex, opposite edges, a few nebulae and asteroids, and its own fleet names (Skiffs, Darts, Arrow, Lance, Flower). Keeping the game separate means these ideas can be tried honestly, not sanded down to match Opus.

## The five rough spots, fixed in a Grok way

A playthrough next to Rapid Opus showed where Grok feels unfinished. Each one should be answered in Grok’s own style, not by copying Opus’s screen.

1. **What just happened is easy to miss.** If the computer acts out of sight, the log often just repeats “The enemy acted out of your sight.” A small toast can still say “The computer moves first” after it is already your turn, so the messages argue with the clock. *Grok’s answer:* the 3-second watch is the unique idea — make it the moment you actually understand the beat. When they did something you can see, the watch should name it clearly. When they really were out of sight and nothing hit you, say that once, calmly, and move on. Never leave a toast on screen that disagrees with “Your turn.”

2. **Whose turn is it?** The vertical timer does say “Your turn,” but the opening can feel backwards, and there is no strong “now watch them” moment when the watch does run. *Grok’s answer:* keep the timer as Grok’s clock, but make act and watch unmistakable (teal when it is yours, purple-pink when you are watching them). If they did go first, say so before you are asked to move, not on top of your turn.

3. **The controls are blunt.** Move, Laser, Missile and Scan are plain buttons. Laser can show “(0)” and Missile can sit grey with no reason until you hunt for the hint. A 3-hex scout lights up a wide circle of hexes, which is the point of Grok’s movement, but it is a lot to read. *Grok’s answer:* keep “tap a glowing hex and go” — do not add a second confirm tap. Make the circle readable (so the far hexes still feel like a 3-hex reach, not a flood), and put a short plain-English reason on a button that will not fire.

4. **The table looks like a sketch next to the main game.** In landscape the timer is a thin strip on the left, the buttons are a narrow column on the right, and your fleet sits on the right-hand edge. There is no zoom, help or sound, and no sense of “this is your home edge.” *Grok’s answer:* this stays Grok’s own screen, not a copy of the main play screen. Keep the side timer and the side panel if they still serve the fast clock, but make the board feel finished: your teal fleet should have a clear home, forward should feel like forward, and the hexagonal hulls should sit comfortably in the space. Add only what this game needs.

5. **The end report is close, but rough.** It already has swipeable cards (head to head, shots, key moments) and **Replay**, which we should keep. The result line is repeated, Log and Pause linger in the header, and key moments can say “Skiff 1 destroyed” when both sides have a Skiff 1. *Grok’s answer:* tidy the report, name *whose* ship, and let Replay stay the star of the ending.

One small typo lived on the Play menu: it said you get 5 seconds to watch the reply, but the game gives 3. That menu line is corrected in this same change.

## Rebuild order

Small steps, each able to ship on its own. Start with feedback, because that is what makes a beat feel like a game.

1. **What just happened.** Rewrite the beat recap and the out-of-sight line. Make the 3-second watch worth watching. Stop toasts that argue with the clock.
2. **Whose turn.** Make act and watch unmistakable, including the opening when the computer really does go first.
3. **The controls.** Reasons on grey buttons; a readable 3-hex move circle, still one tap to go.
4. **The table.** A finished Grok landscape: timer, home edge, hexagonal fleets, teal versus purple-pink. Still its own screen.
5. **The ending.** Tidy the report, keep Replay, name ships clearly.

No need to add Opus’s line, simultaneous ticks, or “tap to preview, tap again to go.” Those are Opus’s ideas. Grok should stay turn-about, 3-hex, and immediate.

## What could later come back to the main game

These are worth borrowing once they feel good in Rapid Grok — and not before Ankur has played the rebuild and said so:

- **Replay** of a finished fight, beat by beat.
- **Tap to skip the watch**, and skip it entirely when there is nothing to see, so the pace stays honest.
- **3-hex circle moves** for small ships — a different pace from one hex at a time.
- A **turn-about** rapid (you, then them) as a second feel beside both-sides-at-once.
- A log that speaks in **beats**, in plain English.
- The short red **Cancel** when the clock is about to fire for you.
