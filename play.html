Turn your phone sideways

Shatkon plays best with the phone held sideways: the whole board fits and the buttons sit under your thumbs.

How to play Shatkon

  1. Goal: destroy the enemy flagship (marked ★). Each player has a small scout and a big flagship, built from hex segments full of parts.
  2. Your turn: you get 3 command points. Moving one ship, firing one laser, launching one missile or scanning costs 1 point each. Unspent points are banked (up to 6).
  3. Who moves first in round 1 is picked at random each game and shown when the game starts (story chapters: you always start). After that the first move swaps every round, so whoever moves last in a round moves first in the next: a double turn, and it goes to each side in turn. The strip at the top shows the round step by step.
  4. Your ships: a ship with a white rim round its hexes is yours to command. Tap it and its panel opens with its orders (move, fire, launch, scan) and its key numbers. A ship without the rim can't take orders right now, so tapping it opens nothing. The view only moves when you move it: drag to pan, pinch or use the mouse wheel to zoom, double-tap one of your ships to zoom in on it and double-tap it again to zoom back out. ⛶ shows the whole board.
  5. Moving: tap your ship, tap a green marker to preview, then tap it again. Each ship has its own thrust: its engines add 1 each a turn, and a hex costs 1 per segment, so a big ship with few engines saves up for a turn or two. Pick a ship to see its thrust bar. A red ring on a marker means an enemy you can see could shoot your ship there on its next turn; the ship panel says how hard ("In enemy reach −3").
  6. Fog of war: you only see what your ships can detect. Big ships are easier to spot, stealth parts and the purple nebula hide you, and firing or launching makes you flare up. A "?" marks where an enemy was last seen.
  7. Lasers hit instantly in a straight line: 3 hits up to 4 hexes, 2 hits up to 8, 1 hit at 9, and +1 if the ship moved at least 1 hex earlier in the turn. Asteroids and your own ships block them. Press Fire and the board shows your reach in red, with the hits a shot does on every hex. Missiles don't move when you launch them: at the end of each round, after both players have had their turn, missiles fly (6 hexes a round). Enemy missiles you can see are drawn with a red dashed line to where they are heading. Point defence can stop one missile a round.
  8. Detection tiers: a red fuzzy circle means something is somewhere inside it (any hex in the circle is equally likely, and the circle doesn't move while the target sits still, so looking again tells you nothing new); ◆ means you know exactly where a ship is; ▣ means a fresh scan shows every part and its hull.
  9. Scan (1 point, once per ship per turn): a ship can only scan on a turn it has moved (at least 1 hex away from where it started the turn; moving there and back doesn't count). The further forward the ship is, the stronger the scan: near your home edge it sees 2 hexes clearly and 4 faintly, in mid-field 4 / 7, and from the centre line onwards 6 / 10. Before sending it you see both rings and how much further forward the next tier is. Everything in the green ring is fully revealed, hidden mines included; the amber ring only picks up faint contacts. Scanning lights your ship up.
  10. Stealth & signature work like engines and thrust: a ship's signature starts at its size (1 per segment) and each working stealth part takes 1 off, down to a floor of segments ÷ 3 (rounded up). Firing, launching or scanning adds a flare. You're spotted when an enemy is within their sensor range + your signature, so the flagship (7 segments, 4 stealth = 3) is seen from 7 hexes instead of 11. Stealth parts are free; lose one to damage and you lose its −1.
  11. Engine wake: a big move (2+ hexes) leaves a wake (signature +1), so creeping 1 hex at a time is quieter. It lasts until that ship's next turn.
  12. Damage strips the outer layers first: shields, then armour, then weapons, then the guts. Lose the core and the ship is gone. Every hit shows an impact report. Mines sit hidden in no man's land: only a scan finds them.
  13. On maps with a beacon (gold star): start your turn with a ship touching it and no enemy ship touching it, and you score 1. First to the map's target wins; at the round limit the higher score wins. Holding it lights your ship up for everyone, so stealth won't hide you there.
  14. Your turn ends by itself when you run out of points or moves. Tap "Why these points?" to see where every point came from.

Modes (top right): play the computer, pass one device around, or play on two devices by swapping short turn codes. Desktop: mouse wheel zooms, Esc cancels, Ctrl+Z undoes a move that revealed nothing.
